Customer Segmentation Analysis on Brazilian E-commerce Data

The core components of the RFM customer segmentation analysis model.

\

From Descriptive Analysis to Strategic Segmentation

This report presents a strategic analysis of customer behavior on the Olist e-commerce platform. During my internship, I began with an exploratory analysis of this dataset, uncovering broad patterns in customer behavior, product categories, and order dynamics. That foundational work, showcased in a previous dashboard, was essential to my early development as a data analyst.

However, as I progressed in my data science journey, I began to ask deeper questions: Who are the most valuable customers? Which customers are at risk of churning? Can we segment them meaningfully to drive business strategy?

These questions led to the current project: a focused RFM (Recency, Frequency, Monetary) segmentation analysis designed to uncover actionable customer insights. The RFM model provides a robust framework for understanding customer behavior, and the resulting insights offer a clear, actionable strategy to improve retention, cultivate loyalty, and drive long-term revenue growth.

Summary of Key Insights

This analysis represents a natural evolution of my analytical capabilities—from descriptive exploration to predictive segmentation. By building on the insights from my initial dashboard, the RFM model has enabled a deeper understanding of customer behavior and provided the following strategic insights:

  • From One-Off Sales to Retention Opportunity: My initial dashboard revealed a high volume of orders from a large number of customers. The RFM analysis deepened this finding by showing that a significant portion of the customer base consists of one-time buyers, highlighting a critical opportunity for nurturing and conversion.
  • From Top Cities to Targeted Campaigns: While the exploratory analysis showed a high concentration of sales in São Paulo (SP), Rio de Janeiro (RJ), and Minas Gerais (MG), the RFM analysis reveals that these same hubs have a high proportion of "Lost Customers". This actionable insight suggests the need for localized win-back campaigns in these specific regions.
  • From General Satisfaction to Behavioral Patterns: My initial work identified trends in product categories. The RFM analysis advances this by revealing that product preferences vary significantly by segment. For example, Our Champions gravitate toward health & beauty and sports & leisure products—these categories consistently drive high-value purchases and repeat engagement, Loyal Customers show strong preference for furniture & decor, suggesting emotional attachment and long-term satisfaction.While New Customers explore entry-level items like tech accessories and fashion items, indicating trend-driven buying behavior. This enables a more personalized and effective product strategy.
  • From Describing What Happened to What to Do Next: The RFM analysis provides a clear, actionable framework for customer management. It identifies that "At Risk" customers have the highest average monetary value, making them a prime target for reactivation efforts, and that "Champions" are ideal for loyalty programs to maintain their engagement.

Methodology: Building on the Foundation

The previous exploratory analysis provided several key takeaways, including geographic concentration, product diversity, and customer satisfaction friction points. The current RFM segmentation builds on that foundation by introducing behavioral clustering—allowing us to move from “what happened” to “what should we do next.”

The analysis was executed through a structured, data-driven methodology to ensure the integrity of the insights.

  1. Data Acquisition & Integration
    • Imported five datasets: orders, customers, items, products, and category translations.
    • Merged into a unified DataFrame for holistic customer analysis.
  2. Data Cleaning & Preparation
    • Converted timestamps to datetime format.
    • Handled missing values.
    • Calculated total revenue per order.
  3. RFM Metric Calculation
    • Defined a snapshot date (one day after the last purchase).
    • Computed:
      • Recency: Days since last purchase
      • Frequency: Number of orders
      • Monetary: Total spend
  4. Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA)
    • Visualized distributions of RFM metrics.
    • Identified skewness, outliers, and behavioral patterns.
  5. Customer Segmentation
    • Assigned RFM scores using quintiles.
    • Mapped score combinations to intuitive segments:
      • Champions: Recent, frequent, high spenders—ideal for loyalty programs
      • At Risk: High historical spend but poor recency—prime for reactivation
      • Lost Customers: Long inactive, low value—target with win-back campaigns
      • New Customers: Recent but infrequent—need onboarding and nurturing
      • Potential Loyalists: Moderate scores—ready for upselling
  6. Segment Profiling
    • Compared average RFM metrics across segments.
    • Visualized segment proportions, profiles, and behavioral clusters.

Data Sources & Tools

This project utilized several publicly available datasets from Olist and a robust set of tools for data manipulation, analysis, and visualization.

  • Olist E-commerce Public Dataset: A collection of five datasets covering customer orders, product details, and geographic information, made available on Kaggle.
  • Python: The core programming language used for all data processing and modeling.
  • Pandas: Employed for data cleaning, merging, and manipulation of large datasets.
  • Matplotlib & Seaborn: Libraries used to create all the static visualizations, including histograms, bar charts, and heatmaps.
  • Jupyter Notebook: The primary environment for conducting the analysis and presenting the findings in a structured, reproducible format.

Visual Breakdown of Key Findings

The following visualizations were instrumental in transforming complex data into clear, strategic insights.

Distribution of RFM Metrics (Histograms)

  • Recency Insight: The distribution is heavily right-skewed, indicating that a large portion of customers made purchases a long time ago.
  • Only a small subset of customers have purchased recently, suggesting a high risk of churn across the base.
  • Frequency Insight: The majority of customers placed only one order, with a sharp drop-off after that.
  • This reveals low repeat engagement and highlights the need for nurturing strategies to convert one-time buyers into loyal customers.
  • Monetary Insight: Most customers spent relatively little, while a small group of high-value buyers contributed disproportionately to revenue.
  • This long-tail distribution confirms the presence of a few highly profitable customers worth prioritizing.

  • “Potential Loyalists” and “New Customers” show moderate scores, offering growth potential if properly nurtured.

  • Insight: “Champions” dominate across all normalized metrics, making them the most balanced and valuable group.

Segment Performance Across RFM Metrics

3. Product & Geographic Profiling

Top Product Categories by Segment
  • Insight: “Champions” and “Loyal Customers” consistently purchase high-end categories like bed_bath_table, health_beauty, and furniture_decor.
  • “At Risk” and “Lost Customers” previously favored similar categories, suggesting their disengagement may be behavioral rather than preference-based.
  • “New Customers” explore entry-level categories like housewares and sports_leisure—ideal for onboarding promotions.
  • “Potential Loyalists” show interest in productivity-related categories like computers_accessories and office_furniture.
  • Recommendation: Promote top-performing products and bundle niche items for cross-selling opportunities.
Segment Distribution Across Brazilian States
  • Insight: “Lost Customers” are heavily concentrated in São Paulo (SP), Rio de Janeiro (RJ), and Minas Gerais (MG), suggesting regional disengagement.
  • “Champions” are more evenly distributed, indicating high-value customers exist across multiple regions.
  • States with high proportions of “At Risk” or “Potential Loyalists” offer opportunities for localized re-engagement campaigns.
  • The heatmap supports regional targeting and inventory planning.
  • Recommendation: Localize campaigns and inventory planning based on geographic segment strengths.

4. Time-Based Trends

Monthly Segment Evolution
  • Insight: “New Customers” spike during promotional periods, confirming the impact of marketing campaigns.
  • “Lost Customers” rise when engagement drops, highlighting the need for consistent retention efforts.
  • Segment fluctuations over time reveal seasonal patterns and help evaluate campaign effectiveness.
  • Recommendation: Align marketing efforts with seasonal behavior and monitor segment shifts to anticipate churn and growth.

Actionable Recommendations

By transforming raw data into strategic insights, we can implement a targeted, customer-centric action plan. These recommendations leverage the RFM segmentation to not only improve marketing effectiveness but also to build stronger, more profitable customer relationships.

  1. Nurture and Convert One-Time Buyers: The majority of our customer base consists of one-time buyers. We must focus on moving these "New Customers" and "Potential Loyalists" up the value ladder. This can be achieved by launching a welcome campaign series that includes personalized product recommendations, a small discount on their next purchase, and a survey to better understand their needs and preferences.

  2. Re-engage At-Risk and Lost Customers: Our analysis shows that the "At Risk" segment has high historical value, making them a top priority for reactivation. Deploy a high-impact win-back campaign with exclusive offers and emotional messaging. For "Lost Customers," who are highly concentrated in key metropolitan areas, we should test geo-targeted social media campaigns and local advertising to lure them back with tailored incentives.

  3. Reward and Retain Champions: The "Champions" segment represents our most valuable customers. To maintain their loyalty and encourage advocacy, we must reward them. This can be done by launching a tiered loyalty program that offers exclusive perks like early access to sales, free shipping, and personalized bundles based on their demonstrated preference for high-end categories like 'furniture_decor' and 'health_beauty'.

  4. Localize and Personalize Marketing: The geographic and product insights show a clear need to move away from a one-size-fits-all approach. By localizing our marketing efforts to address the regional distribution of key segments and personalizing product promotions based on category preferences, we can increase campaign effectiveness and drive better results.
